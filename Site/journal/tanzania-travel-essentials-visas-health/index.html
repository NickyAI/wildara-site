Tanzania Travel Essentials: Visas, Health and Practicalities

The Wildara Team10 July 20265 min read
In this guideVisas and entryHealth and vaccinationsMalaria precautionsTravel insuranceMoney and paymentsConnectivity and powerFAQs

The practical side of planning a safari is rarely the most exciting part, but getting it right means you can arrive relaxed and ready to enjoy every moment. This guide covers the essentials we are most often asked about, from visas and vaccinations to money and mobile signal. Please treat it as general guidance only. Entry requirements and health advice can change, and they often depend on your nationality and travel history, so always confirm the latest information with official sources before you travel.

Visas and entry

Most visitors to Tanzania need a visa, and many nationalities can apply online in advance through Tanzania's official eVisa portal. Applying early is wise, as processing can take time. Some travellers may be able to obtain a visa on arrival, but rules vary by nationality and can change, so it is best not to rely on this without checking first. Fees, eligibility and documentation differ between countries, so we do not quote them here. Always use the official government portal rather than third-party websites, and check that your passport has sufficient validity, commonly at least six months beyond your travel dates, with blank pages for stamps. Your government's travel advice pages are also a useful reference.

Health and vaccinations

Speak to your doctor or a travel clinic well ahead of your trip, ideally six to eight weeks before departure, so there is time for any recommended vaccinations. They will advise based on your health, your itinerary and the latest guidance. Routine vaccinations should be up to date. Yellow fever deserves particular attention. A certificate of vaccination may be required for entry if you are arriving from, or have transited through, a country where yellow fever is present. Requirements can change, so confirm your situation with your travel clinic and official sources before you go. Carry any certificates with your passport, as they may be checked on arrival or when travelling on to Zanzibar.

Malaria precautions

Malaria is present in many parts of Tanzania, including safari areas and Zanzibar, though risk varies with altitude and season. Your doctor or travel clinic can advise whether antimalarial medication is appropriate for you and which type suits your health and itinerary. Alongside any medication, avoiding mosquito bites is the most effective protection. Simple, consistent precautions make a real difference every evening.

  • Seek medical advice on antimalarials before you travel
  • Use an effective insect repellent, especially from dusk onwards
  • Wear long sleeves and trousers in the evenings
  • Sleep under a mosquito net where provided, or in screened rooms
  • Seek medical attention promptly if you develop a fever during or after your trip

Travel insurance

Comprehensive travel insurance is essential for any safari and is not included in our prices. Your policy should cover medical treatment, emergency evacuation and repatriation, as many safari areas are remote and the nearest major hospital may be some distance away. Cancellation and curtailment cover is equally important, particularly given that booking deposits are non-refundable. Check that your policy covers any activities you plan to take part in, such as a hot-air balloon flight or walking safari, and that it reflects the full value of your trip. Arrange insurance at the time you book, so cancellation cover applies from the outset, and keep a copy of your policy details with you.

Money and payments

Tanzania's currency is the Tanzanian shilling. US dollars are widely accepted across the tourism industry, particularly at lodges and for tips. Older dollar notes, and notes that are torn or heavily marked, are often refused, so bring clean, recent notes in a mix of denominations. Cash machines can be found in Arusha and larger towns, but not in the parks, so plan ahead before heading out on safari. Many lodges and hotels accept credit cards, sometimes with a surcharge, though acceptance varies. Let your bank know you are travelling, and keep a small amount of shillings for local purchases at markets and roadside stalls.

Connectivity and power

Mobile coverage is good in Arusha and many towns, and increasingly available in parts of the parks, though it can be patchy in remote areas. Many lodges offer Wi-Fi, sometimes only in shared areas and often at slower speeds than you may be used to. A local SIM card or an eSIM can be a convenient option if your phone supports it. The most common sockets are the British-style three-pin Type G, though other types may be found, so a universal adapter is recommended. Our vehicles have charging points, and some camps rely on solar power, so a power bank is useful. Treat limited connectivity as a gift: the plains are best enjoyed with your eyes, not your screen.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Tanzania?

Most visitors need a visa, and many nationalities can apply online in advance through Tanzania's official eVisa portal. Requirements, fees and processing times vary by nationality and can change, so check the official portal and your own government's travel advice well before you travel. Avoid unofficial websites that charge additional fees for the same service.

Is a yellow fever vaccination required?

It depends on your travel history. A yellow fever certificate may be requested if you are arriving from, or have transited through, a country where yellow fever is present. Rules can change and may also apply when travelling on to Zanzibar, so confirm your individual situation with your doctor or travel clinic and official sources before departure.

Do I need to take malaria tablets?

Malaria is present in many parts of Tanzania, including the safari circuit and Zanzibar. Whether you should take antimalarial medication, and which type, is a decision for you and your doctor or travel clinic, based on your health and itinerary. Whatever you decide, using repellent, covering up in the evening and sleeping under nets are all important.

Can I use US dollars in Tanzania?

Yes, US dollars are widely accepted in the tourism industry, including at many lodges and for tips. Bring clean, recent notes, as older or damaged notes are often refused. The local currency is the Tanzanian shilling, which is useful for small purchases in towns and markets. Cash machines are available in Arusha but not inside the parks.
