The Great Migration Month by Month

The Wildara Team28 October 20266 min read

Every year, vast numbers of wildebeest, joined by zebra and gazelle, follow the rains in a great loop through the Serengeti ecosystem and into Kenya's Maasai Mara. There is no starting line and no finish, only a continuous journey shaped by grass, water and instinct. Understanding where the herds are likely to be at different times of year is the key to seeing them at their best. Here, month by month, is how the migration usually unfolds, with the honest caveat that nature keeps its own calendar.

What the migration really is

The Great Migration is the movement of well over a million wildebeest, along with hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, in search of fresh grazing. The herds follow the rains, which bring new grass, and the rains in turn shift through the year. The result is a broadly clockwise circuit through the Serengeti that repeats year after year. It is important to remember that the migration is not a single column of animals on a fixed timetable. Herds split, merge, linger and double back. The timings below are well-established patterns, but rainfall varies, and a good guide reads the conditions daily rather than relying on the calendar alone. That flexibility is one of the great advantages of travelling privately.

January to March: calving in the south

From around January to March, the herds gather on the short-grass plains of the southern Serengeti and the Ndutu area, where the grass is rich in minerals after the short rains. This is calving season. Within a few weeks, a huge number of calves are born, many of them able to stand and run within minutes of birth. So much new life attracts predators. Lion, cheetah, hyena and jackal are all active, and sightings can be intense and unforgettable. The open plains make viewing easier, and the green landscape with its scattered acacias is wonderfully photogenic. February is often considered the heart of calving, though the exact peak varies with the rains.

April and May: the long rains

As the long rains set in, roughly from April into May, the herds begin to drift north and west from the southern plains. The movement can be gradual and scattered, with long lines of wildebeest crossing the central Serengeti as the grass in the south is grazed down. This is the quietest period for visitors. Some camps close, roads can be muddy and wildlife can be harder to pin down. Yet those who travel now are often rewarded with green landscapes, dramatic skies and the sense of having the plains almost to themselves. It suits flexible travellers who value solitude over certainty.

May to June: central and western Serengeti

By around May and June, large concentrations of wildebeest move through the central Serengeti and into the western corridor, an area often associated with the Grumeti River. This is also a time of rutting, when males compete noisily for females, and the plains echo with grunting calls. The Grumeti offers its own river crossings, usually on a smaller scale than the Mara, and its crocodiles are famously large. The central Serengeti, with its resident lion prides and leopards along the rivers, makes an excellent base at this time, combining migration sightings with outstanding year-round game viewing.

July to early October: the Mara River

From around July to early October, the herds reach the northern Serengeti, where the Mara River stands between them and the grasslands beyond. The crossings are the most famous spectacle of the migration: thousands of animals gathering on the bank, hesitating, then plunging into the current while crocodiles wait below. Crossings cannot be predicted to the hour. Herds may approach the water several times before committing, and patience is essential. Spending several nights in the north gives you the best chance of witnessing one. Our 12-day Mara River migration safari is designed around exactly this, allowing time for the drama to unfold rather than rushing between parks.

November and December: the return south

As the short rains arrive, usually around November, the herds turn south again, travelling through the eastern Serengeti and back towards the southern plains. The movement can be fast and purposeful, with long columns of animals stretching towards the horizon. By December, many herds are approaching the Ndutu area once more, ready for the next calving season, and the cycle begins again. This is a rewarding time to travel, combining fresh green grass, migrant birds and the sight of the herds on the move, often with fewer vehicles than the peak months.

Planning around the herds

The best migration safaris are built with flexibility at their core. Rather than chasing a single moment, we choose camps and routes that place you near the likely herds while still offering excellent wildlife if they move unexpectedly. It is a balance of science, local knowledge and a healthy respect for the unexpected, and these are the principles we follow when planning.

  • Allow at least three nights in the migration area to give yourself the best chance
  • Choose your season by experience: calving for predators, the Mara for crossings
  • Combine the migration with the Ngorongoro Crater and Tarangire for variety
  • Pack patience: some of the best sightings come after a long, quiet wait
  • Ask us about current conditions before you book, as patterns shift year to year

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to see a Mara River crossing?

Crossings in the northern Serengeti usually take place from around July to early October, with August and September often considered the most reliable months. Even then, crossings depend on the herds and cannot be scheduled. Staying several nights in the north, with a guide who knows where to wait, greatly improves your chances of seeing one.

Can I see the migration all year round?

The herds are always somewhere in the wider Serengeti and Maasai Mara ecosystem, so there is usually a chance to see them. However, their location changes, and some periods, such as the long rains, can make them harder to find. Planning your route around the likely position of the herds at your time of travel is key.

Is the calving season as good as the river crossings?

Many experienced safari travellers would say it is just as rewarding, simply different. Calving offers huge numbers of animals on open plains, newborn calves and a great deal of predator activity. River crossings are more concentrated drama. Your choice depends on your interests, your travel dates and whether you prefer green landscapes or the golden dry season.

How many days do I need for a migration safari?

We suggest at least three nights in the migration area itself. Combined with the Ngorongoro Crater and perhaps Tarangire, a trip of seven to twelve days works well. Our longer itineraries allow time to wait at the river or follow the herds without feeling rushed, which often makes the difference between a glimpse and a great sighting.
