Safari and Zanzibar: The Perfect Bush and Beach Journey

The Wildara Team5 August 20265 min read
In this guideWhy the combination worksGetting from the bush to the coastHow to plan your daysStone Town, spice and historyBeaches, reefs and the seaWhen to goFAQs

After days of early starts, dusty tracks and the thrill of the plains, there is something deeply satisfying about trading the Land Cruiser for warm turquoise water and the slow creak of a dhow under sail. Zanzibar, the spice island off Tanzania's coast, is the natural partner to a northern safari. It offers the perfect place to rest, reflect on everything you have seen and explore a culture shaped by centuries of trade across the Indian Ocean. Here is how we help guests bring bush and beach together.

Why the combination works

A safari is wonderful, but it is not always restful. Early mornings, long days of concentration and the sheer intensity of the experience can leave even the keenest traveller ready for a change of pace. Zanzibar offers exactly that, with soft beaches, gentle sea breezes and nothing more pressing than choosing between a swim and a nap. The two experiences also complement each other beautifully. The wild, open landscapes of the Serengeti and the Ngorongoro Crater give way to an island of coral reefs, spice farms and winding old streets. Together they create a journey that feels complete, with adventure and rest in equal measure.

Getting from the bush to the coast

Zanzibar is reached by a short domestic flight from Arusha, making the transition from safari to beach remarkably easy. Most guests finish their safari, return to Arusha and fly to the island the same day or the following morning. Flights from some airstrips in the Serengeti may also be possible depending on schedules. Domestic flights often use smaller aircraft with strict baggage allowances, usually requiring soft bags and limited weight. We include the details in your itinerary, and we recommend checking the current allowance before you pack. Arranging the flight as part of your safari means timings, transfers and connections are all taken care of.

How to plan your days

There is no single right balance between bush and beach, but most guests find that a few nights on the island is the perfect way to end their journey. Several of our itineraries combine a full safari with time in Zanzibar, ranging from around nine to eleven days in total. Here are a few principles we follow when shaping a combined trip.

  • Allow at least five or six days on safari to cover the key northern parks
  • Add three to five nights in Zanzibar for genuine rest
  • Consider a night in Stone Town before heading to the beach
  • Leave a little flexibility around flights, rather than squeezing every hour
  • End with the beach rather than starting with it, so you leave relaxed

Stone Town, spice and history

Stone Town, the historic heart of Zanzibar City, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a fascinating place to explore on foot. Its narrow lanes are lined with carved wooden doors, coral-stone houses, mosques, churches and bustling markets, reflecting the island's Swahili, Arab, Indian, Persian and European influences. A guided walk brings its layered history to life, from the old fort to the former slave market site, now a place of remembrance. Many guests also enjoy a visit to a spice farm, where cloves, nutmeg, cinnamon and vanilla grow, a reminder of why Zanzibar earned its name as the spice island. In the evening, the Forodhani Gardens waterfront fills with food stalls and life.

Beaches, reefs and the sea

Zanzibar's beaches are famously beautiful, with fine white sand and water in every shade of blue. The character of the coast varies around the island. On parts of the east coast, the tide retreats a long way at low water, revealing seaweed farms and rock pools, while the north is known for deeper water that is good for swimming at most times of day. Snorkelling and diving trips visit reefs around the island and its smaller neighbours, where colourful fish and coral can be seen. Sunset dhow cruises are a lovely way to end a day, and many guests simply enjoy the luxury of doing nothing at all after an active safari.

When to go

The best times for a bush and beach journey are generally the drier months, roughly June to October and January to February, when conditions suit both the safari parks and the coast. The long rains of around April and May bring heavier rainfall to the island as well as the mainland, and some beach properties close for part of this period. As ever, weather patterns vary from year to year, and we are happy to advise on the best timing for your particular plans.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get from my safari to Zanzibar?

Zanzibar is reached by a short domestic flight from Arusha. Most guests finish their safari, return to Arusha and fly to the island the same day or the next morning. We can arrange the flight and transfers as part of your itinerary, so connections are seamless. Light aircraft have strict luggage rules, so please check the allowance before packing.

How many nights should I spend in Zanzibar?

Three to five nights is a good balance for most travellers. This allows time for a guided walk in Stone Town, a spice farm visit or snorkelling trip and, most importantly, a few days of genuine relaxation on the beach. Longer stays are easy to arrange if you want to unwind fully before flying home.

Is Zanzibar suitable for families?

Yes. Many beach hotels welcome families, and gentle activities such as rock-pooling, snorkelling and dhow trips are enjoyed by children and adults alike. On parts of the east coast the tide goes out a long way, so families who want easy swimming at any time may prefer the north. We can suggest family-friendly properties.

What should I wear in Stone Town?

Zanzibar is a predominantly Muslim island, and modest dress is respectful in Stone Town and villages. Covering shoulders and knees is appreciated, and a light scarf or sarong is useful. Swimwear is perfectly fine on hotel beaches and at the pool, but cover up when walking through town or visiting local communities.
