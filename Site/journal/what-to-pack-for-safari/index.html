What to Pack for a Tanzania Safari

The Wildara Team8 October 20266 min read

Packing for safari is less about having the perfect wardrobe and more about being comfortable from a chilly dawn to a warm afternoon. You will spend much of each day in a vehicle, stepping out at lodges, picnic spots and viewpoints, so practical clothing matters more than fashion. Laundry is available at many lodges, which means you can pack lighter than you think. Here is what we suggest bringing, based on years of watching guests discover what they used every day and what never left their bag.

Colours and fabrics

Neutral, earthy colours are the safari standard for good reason. Khaki, olive, sand, beige and soft browns blend into the landscape, show dust less and are less likely to disturb wildlife. Very bright colours can be distracting, and white shows the red dust of the plains within an hour. Dark blue and black are best avoided in some areas because they are thought to attract tsetse flies.

  • Choose khaki, olive, sand, stone and muted greens
  • Avoid bright white, which quickly shows dust
  • Avoid dark blue and black where tsetse flies are present
  • Pick breathable, quick-drying fabrics such as cotton blends or technical travel wear
  • Camouflage patterns are best left at home, as they are associated with the military

Layers for cold mornings and warm afternoons

Temperatures can vary a great deal in a single day. Early game drives with the roof raised can feel cold, particularly in the highlands around the Ngorongoro Crater, while the middle of the day on the Serengeti plains can be hot. The answer is layers you can add or remove as the day unfolds. A soft, packable layer earns its place in any daypack.

  • Two or three lightweight long-sleeved shirts for sun and insect protection
  • Several T-shirts or light tops
  • Comfortable trousers, plus shorts if you like them
  • A warm fleece or light down jacket for early mornings and evenings
  • A light waterproof or windproof jacket, especially in the rainy months
  • A wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses and a light scarf or buff for dust
  • Comfortable closed shoes or light walking boots, plus sandals for the lodge

Bags and luggage limits

If your itinerary includes a domestic flight, for example to the northern Serengeti or to Zanzibar, you are likely to fly in a light aircraft. These planes have small holds and strict luggage rules. Soft, flexible bags without rigid frames or wheels are usually required, as they can be squeezed into tight spaces. Weight limits are often around 15 kilograms per person including hand luggage, but this varies by airline, so always confirm the exact allowance before you pack. Even on a road safari, a soft duffel is easier to load into the Land Cruiser than a large hard suitcase. If you are continuing elsewhere after your safari, hotels in Arusha can often store extra luggage for you.

Binoculars, cameras and power

Our vehicles carry binoculars for guests, but many travellers like to bring a personal pair so they never have to share at a key moment. A magnification of 8x or 10x is ideal for safari. For photography, a camera with a good zoom will bring distant animals closer, though modern phones capture wonderful landscapes and close sightings. Whatever you bring, keep it light and easy to reach.

  • A personal pair of binoculars, 8x42 or 10x42 are popular choices
  • A camera with a zoom lens, plus spare batteries and memory cards
  • A soft beanbag or small cushion to steady a long lens on the vehicle
  • A dust-proof bag or cover for camera gear
  • Charging cables and a universal travel adapter
  • A portable power bank for long days between charging points

Our Land Cruisers have charging points, so you can top up batteries while driving between sightings. Even so, a fully charged spare battery is the best insurance against missing the moment a leopard finally climbs down from its tree at dusk. Label your cables, keep them in one pouch and plug in whenever you return to the lodge, so nothing is left behind on the last morning.

Health, sun and comfort

The equatorial sun is strong even when it feels mild, and the combination of wind and dust can be drying. A small personal kit makes long days far more comfortable. If you take prescription medication, bring enough for the full trip plus a few extra days, kept in your hand luggage. Keep the essentials in a small daypack within reach.

  • High-factor sunscreen and lip balm with SPF
  • Insect repellent suitable for tropical use
  • Any prescribed antimalarials, following advice from your doctor or travel clinic
  • A small first-aid kit with plasters, painkillers and rehydration sachets
  • Hand sanitiser and wet wipes
  • A reusable water bottle to refill from the mineral water in the vehicle
  • A small daypack for the vehicle with your essentials

What you can leave at home

Most guests pack too much. Laundry is available at many lodges, usually for a small charge, so a few days of clothing is generally enough even for a longer trip. Formal evening wear is not needed: lodges are relaxed, and a clean shirt and trousers are perfectly appropriate for dinner. Leave valuable jewellery at home, and remember that single-use plastic carrier bags are banned in Tanzania, so pack with reusable or alternative bags. Pack a swimsuit if your lodge has a pool or if Zanzibar is part of your journey, and a good book for the quiet midday hours.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need neutral-coloured clothing?

It is strongly recommended. Neutral colours such as khaki, olive and sand show dust less, stay cooler in the sun and are less likely to disturb wildlife. Dark blue and black can attract tsetse flies in some areas, and bright white quickly looks grubby. You do not need specialist safari clothing, simply comfortable items in muted tones.

Can I bring a hard suitcase?

On a road safari it is possible, though a soft bag is easier to load into the vehicle. If your trip includes a light aircraft flight, soft bags without rigid frames or wheels are usually required, and weight limits are strict, often around 15 kilograms including hand luggage. Check the exact rules with the airline before you travel.

Will I be able to charge my phone and camera?

Yes. Our Land Cruisers are fitted with charging points, and lodges and camps provide charging facilities in rooms or in a central area. Some remote camps rely on solar power, so it is wise to carry a power bank and a universal adapter, and to charge devices whenever the opportunity arises.

Should I bring my own binoculars?

Binoculars are provided in our vehicles, but many guests enjoy having their own pair so everyone can watch at the same time. A pair of 8x42 or 10x42 binoculars offers a good balance of magnification, brightness and weight. They make a remarkable difference for birdwatching and for spotting animals resting far across the plains.
