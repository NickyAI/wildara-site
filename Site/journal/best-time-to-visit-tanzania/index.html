The Best Time to Visit Tanzania: A Month by Month Guide

The Wildara Team15 November 20266 min read

Ask anyone who lives in Arusha when to go on safari and you will usually get a smile and a question in return: what do you most want to see? Tanzania rewards visitors in every month, but the experience changes with the rains, the grass and the movement of the herds. Golden dry-season afternoons, emerald plains full of newborn wildebeest, quiet green months with soft light and few vehicles: each has its own character. This guide walks through the year so you can choose the season that suits you.

The shape of the Tanzanian year

Northern Tanzania sits just south of the equator, so the seasons are defined less by temperature than by rainfall. Broadly, there are two rainy periods: the short rains, which tend to arrive around November and December, and the long rains, which usually fall from around April into May. Between them lie the drier months. The longest and most popular dry spell runs from roughly June to October, with a shorter, warmer and often drier window from January to March. Weather patterns vary from year to year, so treat these as tendencies rather than fixed dates.

Altitude matters too. The Ngorongoro highlands and the crater rim can feel genuinely cold at dawn, while Tarangire and the Serengeti plains are warm by mid-morning. A good safari plan takes both into account, which is why we always talk about the time of year before we talk about the itinerary. Lake Manyara and Tarangire sit lower and feel warmer, so a single trip can span several climates in a week.

January to March: green season and calving

After the short rains, the southern Serengeti and the Ndutu area turn green and the great herds gather on the short-grass plains to give birth. Calving usually peaks around February, when thousands of wildebeest calves can be born in a matter of weeks. Predators follow closely, and this is one of the most rewarding times of year for big cat sightings and dramatic behaviour. The weather is generally warm, with sunny mornings and the chance of an afternoon shower. Landscapes are lush, skies are often clear and photographers love the light. This is also a popular period, particularly around the holidays and in February, so booking early is wise for the best-placed camps.

April and May: the long rains

The long rains bring the heaviest and most persistent rainfall of the year. Some roads become muddy, a number of seasonal camps close, and certain areas are harder to reach. For many travellers this is a reason to avoid these months, yet for others it is the quiet secret of the Tanzanian calendar. Planning at this time is about flexibility rather than avoidance.

Rain often falls in heavy bursts rather than all day, and the parks are at their greenest. Vehicles are few, birdlife is superb and lodges that remain open frequently offer lower seasonal rates. If you are flexible, patient and happy with a little unpredictability, the long rains can deliver a deeply personal safari. Our team will tell you honestly which parks and routes work best at this time.

June to October: the classic dry season

As the rains end, the grass dries and thins, and animals concentrate around permanent rivers, swamps and waterholes. Game viewing becomes easier because wildlife is more visible and more predictable. Tarangire is at its finest, with large elephant herds drawn to the Tarangire River, and the Ngorongoro Crater offers reliable sightings throughout. In the Serengeti, the migration moves through the central and western regions around May and June, then into the north, where river crossings on the Mara usually take place from around July to early October. Days are dry and pleasantly warm, nights are cool and mosquitoes are generally fewer. It is the busiest and usually most expensive time to travel, so plan well ahead.

November and December: short rains and renewal

The short rains tend to arrive as brief afternoon storms rather than long downpours. Dust settles, the plains begin to green again, and the herds start their journey south through the eastern Serengeti towards the calving grounds. Migrant birds arrive from the northern hemisphere, adding colour and song to every drive. Early November in particular can be a lovely, lower-key time to travel, with good wildlife and fewer vehicles than the peak months. By late December, demand rises again around the festive season. It is an excellent window for travellers who want a balance of value, atmosphere and the sense of a landscape coming back to life.

Prices, crowds and how to choose

In general terms, accommodation rates follow demand. The dry season and the festive period are the most sought after, while the long rains bring the quietest parks and the lowest rates. Shoulder months often offer the best balance. Whatever you choose, a private safari means you set the pace, and a knowledgeable guide makes the most of every season. Here is a simple way to match the season to your priorities.

  • For river crossings: roughly July to early October in the northern Serengeti
  • For calving and predators: roughly January to March in Ndutu and the southern Serengeti
  • For elephants and classic dry-season viewing: June to October, especially Tarangire
  • For value and solitude: the long rains of April and May, or early November
  • For a bush and beach trip: the drier months suit both the parks and Zanzibar

Frequently asked questions

What is the single best month for a Tanzania safari?

There is no single best month, because it depends on what you want to see. For dramatic river crossings, aim for roughly July to September. For calving and predator action, consider February. For quiet parks and lower rates, look at the long rains or early November. Tell us your priorities and we will suggest the timing and route that suit you best.

Is it worth going on safari during the rainy season?

For many travellers, yes. Rain often falls in short, heavy bursts, leaving plenty of time for game drives. The landscapes are vivid, birdlife is excellent and you will share sightings with far fewer vehicles. Some camps close and some roads become harder going, so it suits flexible travellers. We plan routes carefully around conditions at the time.

How far ahead should I book a dry-season safari?

For the peak months of July to October, and for the festive season, booking well in advance is sensible, often six months to a year ahead if you have particular camps in mind. Popular lodges near the Mara River and in the Ngorongoro area fill quickly. Shoulder and green season trips can usually be arranged with shorter notice.

Will it be cold on safari?

Days are usually warm, but mornings and evenings can be surprisingly cool, especially from June to August and at higher altitudes such as the Ngorongoro Crater rim. Early game drives in an open pop-up roof vehicle can feel chilly. A warm fleece, a light jacket and layers you can remove as the day warms up will keep you comfortable.
