Big Five and Big Cats

Lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino, sought with patience and an expert eye.

Add to my safari Matching itineraries
The experience

The phrase Big Five was coined by hunters to describe the animals most challenging to track on foot: lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino. Today it belongs to photographers and wildlife lovers, and it remains a wonderful way to frame a first safari. Northern Tanzania is one of the finest places on the continent to seek them, with each park offering its own strengths, from the elephant herds of Tarangire to the rhino of the Ngorongoro Crater and the celebrated big cats of the Serengeti.

Yet the most memorable sightings are rarely about ticking a list. They are about the lioness who rises from the grass and stretches before an evening hunt, the leopard draped along a sausage tree branch, or a pair of cheetahs scanning the plains from a termite mound. Our guides are skilled at reading tracks, alarm calls and the behaviour of other animals, and they will give you the context that turns a sighting into an understanding of how these creatures live.

Every game drive is private, in a 4x4 Toyota Land Cruiser with a pop-up roof, binoculars on board and a guide whose only priority is your experience. That freedom matters. You can wait quietly at a den, follow a pride at a respectful distance, or simply switch off the engine and listen. Full days in the parks include picnic lunches in scenic spots, so there is no need to break off a good sighting to race back to camp.

Moment by moment.

The early start

Big cats are most active in the cool of the morning, so you set out at first light. The air smells of dust and dew, birds are calling, and your guide is already scanning the horizon and the tree lines for the faintest movement or a telltale cluster of vultures.

Reading the bush

A fresh paw print in the track, an impala snorting in alarm, a troop of baboons suddenly restless. Your guide pieces together the clues and follows them carefully, explaining what each sign means. Searching becomes part of the pleasure, and every turn of the road brings anticipation.

Face to face

Then the engine falls silent. A few metres away, a lion lifts its head and looks straight past you with amber eyes. Time slows. You notice the scars on its muzzle, the flick of an ear, the quiet confidence of an animal entirely at home. Cameras click, then even they go still.

Stories at sundown

Driving back as the light turns gold, you pass elephants heading to water and buffalo grazing in the long grass. Later, by the fire, your guide shares stories of the prides and individuals they have come to know over the years, and the day takes on a deeper meaning.

FAQs

Before you go.

Plan my safari Ask a question
What exactly are the Big Five?

The Big Five are the lion, leopard, African elephant, African buffalo and rhinoceros. The term originated with hunters, who considered them the most difficult and dangerous animals to track on foot. Today it is used by photographers and safari travellers as a memorable shortlist of Africa's most iconic species. Northern Tanzania offers the opportunity to see all five, particularly when you combine the Serengeti with the Ngorongoro Crater.

Where is the best place to see leopards?

Leopards are secretive and largely nocturnal, so sightings always feel like a privilege. The Central Serengeti, especially along wooded river courses, is one of the most reliable areas in Tanzania. Tarangire also has leopards in its riverine woodland. Your guide will search the branches of large trees, where leopards often rest during the day or store their kills, so do keep your binoculars close.

Will we see cheetahs as well?

Cheetahs favour open grassland where they can use their speed, which makes the Serengeti plains ideal. They are often seen on termite mounds or small rises, scanning for prey, and are usually active in daylight. The Ndutu area during the calving season can also be excellent for cheetah. While no sighting can be promised, the Serengeti offers some of the best opportunities in Africa.

How close can we get to the animals?

Animals in the parks are accustomed to vehicles, so it is often possible to observe them at remarkably close range. Our guides always follow park regulations and keep a respectful distance, never disturbing natural behaviour or blocking an animal's path. With a private vehicle, you can position for the best angle and wait quietly, which often results in more relaxed and natural sightings.

Is a Big Five safari suitable for children?

Yes, and it is often the highlight of a family holiday. Game drives are conducted from the vehicle, and a private safari means the pace, breaks and drive lengths can be shaped around younger travellers. Children love the thrill of spotting animals for themselves through the binoculars on board. We can suggest itineraries with shorter driving days and family-friendly lodges.
