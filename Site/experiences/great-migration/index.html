The Great Migration

Millions of hooves, one ancient rhythm, and a front-row seat on the plains.

Add to my safari Matching itineraries
The experience

There is no single moment called the Great Migration. It is a year-long circuit, a slow and restless loop in which vast herds of wildebeest, accompanied by zebra and gazelle, follow the rains and the fresh grass they bring across the Serengeti ecosystem. Predators shadow them at every stage. To witness it is to understand that the plains are never still, and that what you see on any given morning is one frame of a much longer story that has played out here for countless generations.

Where you go depends on when you travel. Early in the year the herds gather on the short grass plains around Ndutu, where calves arrive in their thousands within a few weeks. Through the middle months they drift west and north, and from around July the drama shifts to the Northern Serengeti, where the Mara River becomes the stage for the crossings that so many travellers dream of seeing. Between these chapters, the Central Serengeti offers superb resident wildlife at any time.

Our role is to place you in the right part of the ecosystem for your dates, then give you the time and freedom to wait. Because every Wildara safari is private, your guide is never rushing to a schedule set by other passengers. If a herd is massing on the riverbank, you can stay. If the light is better an hour later, you can return. Movements shift with the rains each year, so we plan with care and keep the day flexible, letting the animals decide where the story goes next.

How it unfolds

Moment by moment.

First light on the plains

You leave camp in the grey before sunrise, flask of coffee in hand, the roof of the Land Cruiser already raised. Out on the grasslands the first shapes appear: a line of wildebeest moving with quiet purpose, zebra calling to one another, and somewhere ahead, the herd your guide has been tracking.

The gathering

Hours can pass as the herd builds along the riverbank or across a ridge. Animals bunch, hesitate and turn back, then edge forward again. Your guide reads the signs, positioning the vehicle with patience and respect, and you learn to watch the small details: a raised head, a sudden stillness, dust beginning to lift.

The moment it breaks

When it happens, it happens all at once. One animal commits and the rest follow, pouring down the bank or streaming across the plain in a roar of hooves and dust. Crocodiles wait in the water, lions watch from the grass. It is raw, loud and unforgettable, and over far sooner than you expect.

Afterwards

The plains settle. Survivors regroup on the far side, calves search for their mothers, and vultures circle overhead. You share lunch beneath an acacia, comparing photographs and replaying what you saw. Back at camp that evening, the conversation keeps returning to that single, extraordinary crossing.

FAQs

Before you go.

Plan my safari Ask a question
When is the best time to see the Great Migration?

There is no wrong time, only different chapters. January to March brings the calving season around Ndutu, with newborn calves and active predators. From around July to early October, the herds are typically in the Northern Serengeti, where the Mara River crossings take place. The months in between see the herds moving through the central and western areas. Tell us what you most want to see and we will suggest the right dates.

Can you guarantee we will see a river crossing?

No responsible operator can promise a crossing, because the herds decide when and where to move. What we can do is place you in the right area at the right time of year, give you enough nights to wait, and offer a private vehicle so your guide can stay with a herd for as long as it takes. Patience and flexibility are the best tools for witnessing one.

How many days do I need for a migration safari?

We suggest at least five days for a focused migration safari, which allows time to reach the herds and enjoy several full game drives. Many guests combine the migration with Tarangire, Lake Manyara and the Ngorongoro Crater over eight to twelve days, creating a richer journey through the northern circuit. Adding Zanzibar at the end is a popular way to unwind.

Is the calving season worth visiting?

Very much so. The calving season around Ndutu is one of the most rewarding times in the Serengeti ecosystem. Thousands of calves are born within a few weeks, the plains are green after the short rains, and predators are drawn in close. It is a quieter, more intimate side of the migration, and a wonderful choice for those who love animal behaviour.

Do we drive or fly to see the migration?

Both options work. Driving through Tarangire, the Ngorongoro highlands and into the Serengeti is part of the adventure, with wildlife along the way. If you want to reach the Northern Serengeti quickly, domestic flights can be arranged on request as a supplement. Many guests drive in and fly out, which balances discovery with comfort and time.
