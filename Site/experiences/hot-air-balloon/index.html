Hot-Air Balloon Safari

Drift silently over the Serengeti as the sun rises and the plains wake below.

Add to my safari Matching itineraries
The experience

Few experiences change the way you see Africa quite like a balloon flight. Long before the first game drive vehicles appear, you rise into the cool morning air as the sun lifts over the horizon. Below, the Serengeti unfolds in every direction: winding river courses traced by trees, herds scattered like brushstrokes across the grass, and the long shadows of acacias stretching towards the west. From above, the scale of the ecosystem finally makes sense.

What surprises most people is the quiet. Between the occasional roar of the burner, there is only the soft rustle of wind and the sounds drifting up from the ground: the call of a bird, the snort of a wildebeest, the bark of a zebra. Balloons fly with the breeze, so each flight follows its own path, and the pilot will adjust altitude to bring you low over a herd or high enough to take in the full sweep of the plains.

A balloon safari is available as an optional supplement on request, and is typically arranged in the Central Serengeti, with seasonal flights in other areas. We coordinate everything with your itinerary, including the early pick-up from your camp, so all you need to do is set an alarm and dress warmly. After landing, a bush breakfast is usually served out on the plains, and your guide meets you to continue the day's game drive.

How it unfolds

Moment by moment.

Before dawn

Your pick-up comes in darkness, the stars still bright above the camp. At the launch site, crews work by torchlight as the great envelope fills with air, glowing amber each time the burners fire. There is a short safety briefing, a ripple of excitement, and then you climb into the basket.

Lift-off

The ground falls away so gently you barely notice it. Suddenly you are level with the treetops, then above them, and the eastern sky is turning pink and gold. The burner roars, then falls silent, and the Serengeti stretches out beneath you in the soft, flattering light of a new day.

Over the herds

The pilot brings you low across the plains. Giraffes look up from the treetops, wildebeest file along their well-worn paths, and perhaps a hyena trots back towards its den. You see patterns invisible from the ground: game trails, river bends, the shape of the land that guides the animals.

Breakfast in the bush

After a soft landing, the crew gathers the balloon while you step onto the grass, a little elated. A breakfast is usually laid out in the open, often with a glass of something sparkling to mark the flight. Then your guide arrives, and the day continues with a game drive.

FAQs

Before you go.

Plan my safari Ask a question
Is a hot-air balloon safari safe?

Balloon safaris in the Serengeti are run by experienced, licensed operators who follow strict safety procedures. Pilots monitor weather conditions closely and will only fly when conditions are suitable. Every flight begins with a safety briefing explaining how to stand during take-off and landing. As with any adventure activity, listening to the crew's instructions is important, and we only work with established operators.

How early do we need to get up?

Flights launch around sunrise, so pick-up from your camp is usually well before dawn, depending on how far you are staying from the launch site. It is an early start, but most guests agree the reward is more than worth it. We will confirm timings the evening before and can plan a more relaxed afternoon so you have time to rest.

Can children join a balloon flight?

Many balloon operators welcome older children, though each company sets its own minimum age and height requirements. Younger children are usually not permitted for safety reasons. Please share the ages of your travelling party when you enquire, and we will confirm what is possible. If some of the family cannot fly, they can enjoy a morning game drive and meet you at breakfast.

What happens if the flight is cancelled?

Safety always comes first, and flights may occasionally be postponed or cancelled because of wind or weather. If this happens, the balloon operator will explain the options available, which may include rescheduling where your itinerary allows. Your guide will be ready to take you on a morning game drive instead, so the day is never wasted.

Which part of the itinerary works best for ballooning?

We usually recommend scheduling your flight on a morning when you have at least two nights in the same area of the Serengeti. This avoids a long transfer straight after landing and gives some flexibility if conditions are unsuitable on the first attempt. It also lets you enjoy the full experience, from the quiet dawn launch to the leisurely breakfast afterwards.
