Overview

Arusha sits at the foot of Mount Meru and is widely known as the safari capital of northern Tanzania. It is our home, and it is where most journeys begin and end. The town has a lively, green character, with markets, coffee farms and the mountain often looming through the clouds. Many travellers arrive at Kilimanjaro International Airport, around an hour away, and spend a night here to rest before setting out for the parks.

Just outside town, Arusha National Park is one of the most underrated parks in Tanzania. It is compact but remarkably varied, rising from open grassland to montane forest and the slopes of Mount Meru, at around 4,566 metres the country's second-highest peak. The Momella Lakes, shallow and alkaline, shimmer in shades of green and blue and often attract flamingos. On a clear day, Kilimanjaro appears on the horizon. It is a gentle, beautiful introduction to Tanzania's wild places.

The park is known for black and white colobus monkeys swinging through the forest canopy, giraffes and buffalo grazing in the clearings, and the Ngurdoto Crater, a lush, enclosed bowl viewed from its rim. Walking safaris with an armed ranger are a highlight, offering a closer connection to the landscape. It makes a perfect first or last day, particularly before or after a longer northern circuit safari. It rewards travellers who enjoy detail as much as big game.

    Should I spend a night in Arusha?

    We recommend it. Most international flights arrive at Kilimanjaro International Airport, around an hour from Arusha, and a night here lets you rest before your safari begins. It also gives a buffer if flights are delayed. Many travellers spend a final night in Arusha too, before flying home or onward to Zanzibar.

    Is Arusha National Park worth visiting?

    Yes, especially if you have a spare day at the start or end of your safari. It is very different from the savannah parks: forests, lakes, colobus monkeys and the chance to walk with an armed ranger. It is compact and close to town, so it fits easily into an itinerary without long drives.

    Can I climb Mount Meru?

    Mount Meru, at around 4,566 metres, is a challenging climb that usually takes three to four days and is often used as acclimatisation for Kilimanjaro. Our focus is on safaris, but we are happy to discuss options. Shorter walks on the lower slopes of the park are also possible with a ranger.

    Are there lions or elephants in Arusha National Park?

    Arusha National Park is not a big game destination in the same way as the Serengeti or Ngorongoro. Elephants are present but seldom seen, and lions are not part of the experience. Instead, the park is loved for giraffes, buffalo, colobus monkeys, flamingos and the scenery, with walking safaris as a highlight.

    How do I get to Arusha?

    Kilimanjaro International Airport is the main gateway, around an hour's drive from Arusha. Some regional flights use Arusha Airport, closer to town. Our team will meet you on arrival and transfer you to your hotel, and we can arrange connections to and from Zanzibar or other destinations. Simply share your flight details with us.

    Is Arusha safe for travellers?

    Arusha is a busy, friendly town that sees many visitors. As anywhere, sensible precautions apply: keep valuables secure, use trusted transport and ask your hotel or guide for advice. Our team is based here and will help you settle in, including on a short guided walk if you would like one.
