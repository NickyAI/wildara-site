Overview

After days of dust and early starts, there is no better way to end a safari than on Zanzibar. A short flight from Arusha carries you to an island around 40 kilometres off the Tanzanian coast, where the air is warm and humid, the water is a dozen shades of turquoise and the pace of life slows to the rhythm of the tides. Dhows with patched sails glide past, and the sand is fine and pale underfoot.

Stone Town, the historic heart of the island and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is a labyrinth of narrow lanes, carved wooden doors and coral-stone houses with overhanging balconies. Swahili, Arab, Indian and European influences meet here, in mosques and churches, bustling markets and quiet courtyards. A guided walk reveals the island's layered history, from its role in the spice trade to the darker chapters of its past. Evenings bring the scent of grilled seafood from the waterfront.

Beyond Stone Town, the coast offers something for every mood. Nungwi in the north has a lively, sociable atmosphere and fine sunsets, while Matemwe on the east coast is quieter, facing the reef and the waters around Mnemba Atoll, known for snorkelling. Spice farms, mangrove forests and the red colobus of Jozani Forest add variety. We usually suggest a night or two in Stone Town, followed by several days on the beach.

    How do I get from the safari to Zanzibar?

    Zanzibar is reached by a short domestic flight from Arusha, often in under two hours. Many of our itineraries finish the safari in the morning, then transfer to Arusha Airport for the afternoon flight. On arrival, our driver will meet you for the private transfer to your hotel in Stone Town or on the coast.

    How many nights should I spend on Zanzibar?

    Four to six nights is ideal: one or two nights in Stone Town to explore the history and culture, followed by three or four nights on the beach to relax. Shorter stays are possible, but the island rewards a slower pace, and the transition from safari to beach is part of the joy.

    Which part of Zanzibar is best?

    It depends on your mood. Stone Town is for history, culture and food. Nungwi in the north has lively beaches and easy swimming. Matemwe on the north-east coast is quieter, with views to Mnemba Atoll and good snorkelling. We will suggest the best combination for you. Many travellers enjoy splitting their beach time between two areas.

    When is the best time to visit Zanzibar?

    June to October and December to February are the most popular times, with dry, sunny weather. The long rains in April and May bring frequent showers and some closures. November brings short rains, often in brief bursts. Zanzibar combines well with a safari at almost any time of year. Sea conditions vary by coast.

    Is Zanzibar good for families?

    Yes. The beaches, warm water and snorkelling are a hit with children, and Stone Town's history and spice farms offer variety. We will recommend hotels and beaches suited to families, considering tides and swimming conditions. The short flight from Arusha makes it an easy extension to a family safari. Calm, shallow water suits younger swimmers.

    What should I wear in Zanzibar?

    Light, breathable clothing is best. On the beach, swimwear is fine, but in Stone Town and villages, covering shoulders and knees is respectful, as the island is largely Muslim. A hat, sunglasses, sandals and reef-safe sunscreen are useful. A light layer for evening breezes can be handy. Light cotton and linen work well.
