Lake Eyasi

A remote soda lake where Hadzabe hunters and Datoga smiths share ancient skills.

Overview

Lake Eyasi lies in a remote, arid corner south of the Ngorongoro highlands, tucked between the Eyasi escarpment and the hills to the south. It is a seasonal soda lake whose shoreline shifts with the rains, surrounded by acacia scrub, doum palms and ancient baobabs. The light here is harsh and golden, and the pace slows. After days of game drives, Eyasi offers something different: a chance to meet the people who have lived in this landscape for generations.

The Hadzabe are among the last hunter-gatherers in Africa, living largely from what they hunt and gather in the bush. A visit begins early, when hunters set out with handmade bows and arrows, and you may be invited to walk with them, learning how they track animals, find edible roots and berries and make fire. Their language uses click sounds, and their knowledge of the land is profound. Time spent with them is unhurried and remarkably personal.

Nearby, the Datoga are pastoralists known for their skill as blacksmiths, shaping arrowheads, bangles and ornaments from recycled metal. Visiting a Datoga homestead, you will see their craftsmanship and learn about their traditions. Visits are arranged respectfully through local guides, and we encourage an attitude of curiosity and humility. Eyasi is usually explored as a day trip from Karatu, often combined with the Crater or Manyara. It adds a human dimension that many travellers remember long after the safari ends.

    Is visiting the Hadzabe ethical?

    It can be, when done respectfully. Visits are arranged with local guides and communities, and fees support them directly. We encourage small groups, curiosity and humility, and we ask travellers to follow guidance on photographs and behaviour. The aim is genuine exchange, not a performance, and our guides help ensure the experience is respectful.

    How do I get to Lake Eyasi?

    Lake Eyasi is usually reached from Karatu, a drive of around two hours, the last part on unpaved roads. Most travellers visit as a day trip, returning to Karatu in the afternoon. It fits naturally between the Crater, Manyara and the Serengeti in a northern circuit itinerary. The final stretch is rough but scenic.

    Will I go hunting with the Hadzabe?

    You may be invited to walk with Hadzabe hunters as they track small animals and birds. Whether a hunt is successful is unpredictable, and the focus is on learning about their skills and way of life. The walk can be brisk and through thorny bush, so good footwear and a reasonable level of fitness help.

    Is Lake Eyasi suitable for children?

    Yes, often very much so. Children tend to be fascinated by the Hadzabe and their bushcraft, and the Datoga blacksmiths are a highlight. The walk can be adapted for younger travellers. The road from Karatu is bumpy, and the heat can be tiring, so we plan breaks and plenty of water.

    What should I bring?

    Wear sturdy closed shoes, long trousers, a hat and sunscreen, and bring water. A small backpack is useful. Cash for buying handmade items directly from the Datoga or Hadzabe can be helpful. Neutral clothing is best, and a respectful attitude is the most important thing to bring. A notebook can be useful for remembering the stories you hear.

    Can I see wildlife at Lake Eyasi?

    Eyasi is primarily a cultural experience rather than a wildlife destination. You may see birdlife on the lake, such as flamingos and pelicans, along with smaller mammals and monkeys. For the big game, we pair Eyasi with nearby parks such as the Ngorongoro Crater, Lake Manyara or the Serengeti. Together they make a well-rounded journey.
