Overview

Tarangire is often the first park on a northern Tanzania safari, and it sets the tone beautifully. Just a couple of hours from Arusha, it is a landscape of rolling savannah, acacia woodland and, above all, baobabs: vast, silver-barked trees with swollen trunks and branches like roots reaching for the sky. Some are thought to be many centuries old, and their hollowed trunks carry the scars of generations of elephants searching for moisture.

The park takes its name from the Tarangire River, which flows through it from south to north. When the rains end and the surrounding plains dry out, the river becomes a lifeline, and animals move in from across the wider ecosystem. From roughly June to October the park holds some of the densest concentrations of elephants in Tanzania, and watching a family herd come down to dig for water in the sandy riverbed is a privilege.

Beyond the elephants, Tarangire rewards patience. Lions rest in the shade, leopards use the riverine trees, and herds of zebra, wildebeest, buffalo and impala gather near water. The Silale swamp in the south holds water year round, and the birdlife across the park is outstanding. It combines naturally with Lake Manyara, Ngorongoro and the Serengeti, and staying near the park allows sunrise and sunset drives. We find it a wonderful place to begin, gentle in pace yet rich in detail.

    How long should I spend in Tarangire?

    A full day game drive gives a good introduction, and many of our itineraries begin here before continuing to Karatu in the evening. If you love elephants or birdlife, a night near the park makes a real difference, allowing early morning and late afternoon drives when the light is soft and the animals are most active.

    When is the best time to visit Tarangire?

    June to October is the classic time, when the dry season draws wildlife to the river and elephant numbers are at their highest. The green season from November to March is excellent for birdlife and landscapes, though animals are more dispersed. April and May are the wettest months, when grass is tall and some areas are harder to reach.

    Are there tsetse flies in Tarangire?

    Yes, tsetse flies are found in some wooded parts of the park. Their bites can be irritating but are generally not a health concern for visitors in this area. Wearing neutral, light-coloured clothing with long sleeves, avoiding dark blue and black, and using insect repellent all help. Your guide will close windows in heavily affected areas.

    Can I see the Big Five in Tarangire?

    Elephant, buffalo and lion are seen regularly, and leopards are present, particularly along the river. Rhino are not found in Tarangire. To complete the Big Five, we recommend pairing it with the Ngorongoro Crater, which offers a good chance of seeing black rhino, along with the Serengeti for big cats.

    Is Tarangire good for a short safari?

    Very good. Its proximity to Arusha makes it ideal for one or two day safaris, and it combines easily with Lake Manyara or the Ngorongoro Crater over two to three days. The drive is around two hours on good roads, so you spend more time watching wildlife and less time in transit.

    Is Tarangire suitable for families?

    Yes. The short drive from Arusha suits children, and the elephants are a reliable highlight that keeps young travellers engaged. Game drives can be shortened, with breaks at picnic sites, and staying near the park reduces travel time. We will tailor the pace to the ages and interests of your family.
